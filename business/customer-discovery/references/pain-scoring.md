# Pain Scoring Rubric

Score a discovered pain only after interviews provide evidence. Maximum 100.

| Dimension | Max |
|---|---:|
| Frequency | 15 |
| Financial impact / value at stake | 20 |
| Time consumed | 15 |
| Urgency / consequence of delay | 10 |
| Dissatisfaction with current workaround | 10 |
| Existing spend / demonstrated willingness to solve | 10 |
| Decision-maker awareness | 5 |
| Ease of measuring ROI | 5 |
| Repeatability across target segment | 10 |
| **Total** | **100** |

## Evidence strength

Add a separate evidence label:

- **Strong** — observed records/behavior, current spend, repeated workaround, paid attempt, measurable workflow data.
- **Moderate** — consistent reports from multiple comparable customers with reasonable quantification.
- **Weak** — opinions, hypothetical interest, isolated anecdotes, vague frustration.

## Interpretation

- **80–100:** severe monetizable pain; move toward an offer/payment test if evidence is Strong or Moderate.
- **65–79:** promising pain; quantify remaining economic assumptions.
- **50–64:** secondary pain or insufficient evidence.
- **<50:** do not build around this pain by default.

A high pain score with Weak evidence requires more discovery, not product development.

## Cross-interview signal

Track separately:
- number of comparable interviews;
- number experiencing the pain;
- number who attempted to solve it;
- number currently paying to solve it;
- number willing to take a concrete next step (demo, data access, pilot discussion, proposal, deposit).

Do not invent a universal minimum interview count. Use saturation: continue until additional interviews stop materially changing the pain map, while ensuring enough comparable interviews to avoid overfitting to one customer.
