# Customer Discovery Interview Framework

## Objective

Reconstruct real behavior and economics. Do not conduct a survey disguised as an interview.

## Recommended 20–30 minute sequence

### 1. Context
- What does the company do?
- Team size and roles relevant to the workflow?
- Approximate volume of customers/jobs/interventions/orders per week or month?
- What software/tools are involved?

### 2. Reconstruct a recent real case
Ask the interviewee to choose the most recent relevant case and walk through it chronologically.

For every step capture:
`Trigger -> Person -> Action -> Tool -> Information -> Handoff -> Delay/error -> Outcome`

### 3. Friction
- Which step consumes the most administrative time?
- Where is information entered more than once?
- What regularly arrives incomplete?
- What is often forgotten or delayed?
- Where do employees need to chase someone else for information?
- What requires copying between email, phone, paper, spreadsheets, messaging, and business software?

### 4. Economics
For each significant pain ask:
- frequency;
- time per occurrence;
- people involved;
- approximate loaded hourly cost if known;
- lost/revenue-delayed amount if applicable;
- error/rework cost;
- existing software/service spend.

A rough monthly cost can be estimated as:
`frequency/month × time/occurrence × loaded hourly cost + measurable error/delay costs`

Mark the result as an estimate unless supported by records.

### 5. Existing solution
- How is this handled today?
- Why was that solution chosen?
- What has already been tried?
- Why did previous attempts fail?
- What would make changing the process difficult?

### 6. Buying reality
Only after understanding the pain:
- Who decides whether to buy software or external services for this workflow?
- What typically triggers that purchase?
- What budget category would it come from?
- What proof would be required before adopting a change?

Do not ask hypothetical willingness-to-pay questions as primary evidence. A paid pilot, deposit, or real proposal is a stronger test.

## Closing

Ask:
- 'What should I have asked that I didn't?'
- 'Who else deals with this problem regularly?'

Do not turn the closing into a product pitch unless the interview was explicitly arranged as a sales/discovery hybrid.
