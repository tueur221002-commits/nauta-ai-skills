# Offer Designer

## Purpose
Convert validated customer pain into the smallest credible paid offer that can test willingness to pay. Do not confuse an attractive solution with a validated offer.

## Preconditions
Use this skill only when customer-discovery evidence exists. If pain, segment, frequency, consequence, current workaround, or buyer are mostly assumptions, return to customer discovery.

## Principles
- Sell an outcome, not AI, automation, Make, ChatGPT, or software features.
- Preserve existing tools and behavior when replacement is not necessary.
- Minimize behavior change for end users.
- Prefer one painful workflow and one measurable business result over a broad transformation package.
- A paid pilot, deposit, signed proposal, or real procurement step is stronger evidence than verbal enthusiasm.
- Do not invent ROI, savings, conversion, price tolerance, integration feasibility, or technical capabilities.
- Separate evidence, estimate, assumption, and proposed experiment.
- Do not build the full solution before willingness to pay is tested.

## Workflow

### 1. Evidence gate
Summarize the discovery evidence:
- ICP and relevant operating characteristics
- recurring pain
- frequency
- time/cost/cash-flow impact
- current workaround
- previous attempts and why they failed
- user/champion/economic buyer
- contradictory evidence and solved cases

Classify each material claim as Evidence, Estimate, Assumption, or Unknown.

### 2. Select the wedge problem
Choose the narrowest problem that is:
- frequent
- costly or consequential
- recognized by the buyer
- measurable before/after
- repeatable across the ICP
- solvable without excessive implementation risk

Do not bundle unrelated pains merely to increase perceived value.

### 3. Define the outcome
Express the offer as a business outcome.

Preferred form:
`We help [ICP] achieve/reduce [measurable outcome] in [scope/timeframe], without [major adoption cost or unwanted change].`

Avoid promises unsupported by evidence.

### 4. Design the mechanism
Describe the minimum mechanism required to produce the outcome. Keep technology implementation-agnostic until feasibility is checked.

For workflow offers, map:
Trigger -> Capture -> Structure -> Completeness check -> Human validation -> Existing system -> Business outcome.

Identify what remains manual during the pilot.

### 5. Define the paid pilot
Specify:
- one workflow
- one team/site when possible
- duration
- included implementation
- client responsibilities
- excluded scope
- baseline measurement
- success metrics
- review point
- exit/rollback path

A pilot should be small enough to deploy quickly but meaningful enough to prove economic value.

### 6. Build ROI hypothesis
Use observed client numbers where available.

Potential value pools:
- administrative hours recovered
- previously unbilled labor/materials captured
- faster invoice issuance
- reduced rework/errors
- reduced rejected invoices
- working-capital/DSO improvement

Show formulas and ranges. Never present estimates as realized savings.

### 7. Create pricing tests
Generate 2-3 defensible pricing hypotheses, not a single asserted market price. Consider:
- fixed paid pilot
- setup + recurring fee
- recurring fee after proven pilot
- value-linked component only when measurable and auditable

For each hypothesis state what must be true for the buyer to accept it and what evidence would falsify it.

### 8. Reduce buyer risk
Use legitimate risk reduction:
- narrow pilot
- explicit success criteria
- human validation
- no forced replacement of current core software
- data/privacy/security boundaries
- rollback plan
- clear scope and timeline

Do not promise impossible guarantees.

### 9. Design the willingness-to-pay test
Create a real commercial next step:
- paid pilot proposal
- deposit
- signed letter/order form
- procurement/technical validation step

Do not use `Would you buy this?` as primary validation.

### 10. Verdict
Return one of:
- OFFER READY TO TEST
- NEEDS MORE DISCOVERY
- NEEDS FEASIBILITY CHECK
- REJECT OFFER

Explain the decisive evidence and the next cheapest experiment.

## Required output
1. Evidence gate
2. ICP
3. Wedge problem
4. Offer statement
5. Pilot design
6. Before/after metrics
7. ROI hypothesis with formulas
8. Pricing hypotheses
9. Objections and risk reduction
10. Willingness-to-pay test
11. Assumptions/unknowns
12. Verdict and next experiment
