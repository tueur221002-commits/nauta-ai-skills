# Paid Pilot Framework

## Objective
Test whether the buyer will pay for a narrowly defined business outcome and whether the mechanism can produce measurable value in the real workflow.

## Pilot specification
Document:
- Customer / ICP
- Current workflow and baseline
- One target workflow
- Pilot duration
- Users involved
- Existing systems touched
- Inputs and outputs
- What is automated
- What remains human-validated
- Client responsibilities
- Provider responsibilities
- Explicit exclusions
- Data/security constraints
- Rollback procedure

## Baseline metrics
Capture before implementation whenever applicable:
- average intervention-to-invoice delay
- administrative hours/week spent reconciling intervention data
- percentage of intervention records incomplete
- number/value of forgotten labor or materials
- invoice rejection/rework rate
- DSO or payment delay where causally relevant

## Success metrics
Choose 1 primary metric and at most 2-3 secondary metrics. Avoid a dashboard of vanity metrics.

Example structure:
- Primary: reduce median intervention-to-invoice delay from baseline X to target Y.
- Secondary: reduce reconciliation time; improve completeness; recover otherwise missed billables.

Targets are hypotheses until agreed with the customer.

## Commercial test
A strong test requires a real commitment. In descending strength:
1. paid pilot / signed order
2. deposit
3. signed proposal with procurement action
4. technical/data-access commitment with named owner
5. scheduled follow-up only
6. verbal enthusiasm

Do not treat levels 5-6 as willingness-to-pay validation.

## Pilot review
At the end compare baseline vs observed results, document failure modes and adoption friction, calculate realized value conservatively, and decide:
- SCALE
- ITERATE
- STOP

Do not extrapolate one successful pilot to the whole market without additional evidence.
